Simply put, humans are terribly complicated creatures. There are several different mechanisms going on inside and outside of the human brain, with several different purposes, motivations, and emotions. We tend to get things done, to have a successful life, to love or hate, to cry or be happy. We ourselves want to make sense of our minds, and we have tried to wrap our heads around it. Many different systems have been developed in the past to try to deduce what exactly is going on. Though we have several sound and scientific theories, several others have taken a step further to categorize different personality types for each and every single sapient being. From Isabel Briggs Meyers (MBTI) to Claudio Naranjo (Enneagram) to Aushra Augusta (Socionics) to Alexander Afanasyev (Psychosophy), these human beings pioneered personality typology and brought the subject of personality in a new direction, to mixed results.

And though many have come before the system I am about to share, what you are about to see is a brand-new perspective inspired directly by those pioneers. This system *may* act as a consolidation and combination of all these theories, but the aim of this system is to not just copy them, but to form a comprehensive, standalone, all-encompassing framework to better explain exactly what is going on inside our heads.

# The Initial Idea

The human mind is not a random stream of consciousness, in which major and everyday decisions are made on a whim based on arbitrary pre-determined factors. Rather, there exists **five psychic Forces in five different positions** which drive people and influence their personalities. But before we get into these Forces and positions, there are underlying aspects of the psyche and how we as humans make decisions that drive these Forces.

Deriving information from Carl Jung's theories on personality, we can paint a different picture as to how individuals make decisions. And the picture is this: whenever an individual makes a decision, there are four major factors that they take into account:

- **"What do I think of this?"** This presents itself as an *unbiased, objective opinion* on what the individual is doing.
- **"How do I feel about this?"** This presents itself as an *emotional, subjective opinion* on what the individual is doing.
- **"What even is it that I'm making a decision on?"** This presents itself as *factual, tangible evidence* of what the individual is doing.
- **"Why am I making this decision?"** This presents itself as the *overall future outcome* of what the individual is doing.

These four questions drive our every decision, whether it is something simple as what to eat or where, or a major decision such as the direction that we should take in life. However, it is not enough that we make these decisions on our own; **the world around us itself has a major role to play.** The drive to make these decisions comes from a combination of our own standalone opinions and views; and what the world presents itself as and how we interact and think of it. This forms the basis of the **Human Units** and **Nonhuman Units**, respectively.

# The Human Units

The Human Units represent our own subconscious decisions, independent of how the world presents itself to us. These Human Units drive our decisions by giving us two major perspectives to work with when it comes to them: *logical* and *emotional*.

## The Brain

The Brain is our unit that deals with logical and factual decisions, free of emotional influence. Every decision that does not include emotional elements are made using this Unit.

## The Heart

The Heart is our unit that deals with what we create and what personally and emotionally matters, free from logic or facts. Every decision that does not include logical or factual elements are made using this Unit.

## The Body

There is a third Human Unit that drives the Brain and the Heart towards a common goal, and it is called the Body. In real life, the human brain and heart cannot function by themselves, but require the extra work of a body (among other things) to keep us alive and going. This same exact concept applies to these Human Units. Indeed, every day we have to use both of the first two Units, and occasionally we have to use both of them for one decision. The Body acts as the "glue" to the psyche, and it allows the Brain and the Heart to interact with each other not only when the time comes, but every minute of our lives.

# The Nonhuman Axis and its Poles

The Nonhuman Axis represents two major concepts: what the world gives to us personally; and how we interact with it and create our own views on it, both independent of our own subconscious decisions. It is important to note that **what the world provides to us as individuals is NOT universal; as such, individuals can have entirely different "worlds," so to say.**

## The Objection Pole

On one side of the Nonhuman Axis is the Objection pole, which represents what the world provides to us individually. This comes in the form of how we view the world, free from our own opinions on it. More specifically, it represents a comprehensive snapshot of the entire history of this world: the past, the present, and the future. A question that uses Objection can be in the form of, "What *was/is/can/could/will* be true/workable?"

## The Subjection Pole

On the other side of the Nonhuman Axis is the Subjection pole, which represents our own opinions of the world based on what it gives to us. In the past, we have created our own rules of how the world should work (the realms of logic and philosophy are great examples), and this rings true for Subjection. A question that uses Subjection can be in the form of, "What *should* be true/workable?"

## The Equator

The Equator represents balance; neither Objection nor Subjection thrives or dies here.

# The Energy of the Psyche

Of course, the method in which we exhibit these Human Units and Nonhuman Poles are not random. Instead, depending on the priority of our decision-making methods, all of these Units and Poles have different types of energy. This energy of the psyche consists of two components: the source and the destination; and this energy changes and transfers from the former to the latter.

## The Source

The source represents whether the energy was incited through the outside world or of our own accord.

## The Destination

The destination represents which location the energy is changed and transferred, whether it is to our own minds or the outside world.
