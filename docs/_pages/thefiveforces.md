The Human Units and Nonhuman Axis are the fundamental building blocks of our psyche, as they drive our everyday decisions and motivations. The energy produced and transferred by these aspects can be grouped together and mapped to five psychological elements, or **Forces**, that make up our personality. These five Forces consist of different combinations of Human Units and Nonhuman Poles that form their own psychological aspect that we use daily. Each of these Forces have two main aspects, a Human Element and a Nonhuman Element, as a result of this combination.

These five combinations are as follows:
1. The combination of the **Brain** and **Objection** produces a Force that blends the Human Element of **facts and experiences** with the Nonhuman Element of **the past and present.** This Force is called **Reality**, which is represented as **R.**
2. The combination of the **Brain** and **Subjection** produces a Force that blends the Human Element of **logical systems and consistency** with the Nonhuman Element of **objectivity.** This Force is called **Logic**, which is represented as **L.**
3. The combination of the **Heart** and **Objection** produces a Force that blends the Human Element of **novelty and innovation** with the Nonhuman Element of **the future of the world.** This Force is called **Imagination**, which is represented as **M.**
4. The combination of the **Heart** and **Subjection** produces a Force that blends the Human Element of **personal meaning and significance** with the Nonhuman Element of **subjectivity.** This Force is called **Valuation**, which is represented as **V.**
5. Finally, the combination of the **Body** and **The Equator** produces a special Force that is much more complicated to explain (and will be later). This Force is called **Optimization**, which is represented as **O.**

# The Five Forces in Detail

The following section is a deep dive into each one of the Five Forces. In addition to the main description, there will also be a dedicated section detailing what each Force does NOT entail, using examples from similar concepts from other typology systems.

## The Force of Reality (R)

When an individual imagines "reality", they often think of the concept of an all-encompassing, real world that is unchanging for every single sapient being. There is no changing the facts or what is real, despite what you might believe. You can only change the future; changing the past or the present is futile. And there is no arguing about reality, or the past or present; what you get is what you get. And yet despite this common definition, there is a lesser known one that encompasses the individual's *subjective* perception about what is "real." It is with this definition that we explain what the following Force entails.

The **Force of Reality** combines the Human Unit of the Brain and the Nonhuman Pole of Objection. It involves two major concepts:

- **Human Element:** The formation, understanding, and implementation of **one's own tangible facts, experiences, evidence, and truth, free from logical consistency.**
- **Nonhuman Element:** Knowing and understanding **the current state of the individual's world and how it has changed *in the past*, free from how it *could* or *will* change.**

## The Force of Logic (L)

As humans evolved to become sapient, they developed the capacity for abstract thought. And with this abstract thought came the field of valid reasoning. Logic has actually been around in all periods of human history, but it was only through the work of many logicians such as Aristotle and Chrysippus that logic became a driving force behind everyday decision making and several abstract fields, most notably science and mathematics. Unlike Reality, which uses an uncommon definition of the word, this following Force entails perhaps the most literal definition of its title out of all the Five Forces.

The **Force of Logic** combines the Human Unit of the Brain and the Nonhuman Pole of Subjection. It involves two major concepts:

- **Human Element:** The formation, understanding, and implementation of **one's own logical, sensical, abstract, consistent, and sound systems and theories, free from tangible facts and evidence.**
- **Nonhuman Element:** The formation of **one's own objective opinions based on established rules or facts, free from personal preference.**

**Unorganized thoughts**
- Logic primarily has to do with **the logical rules of the universe;** while Reality tells us *what* our world is like, Logic *explains* it all.
  - "Grass is green." - Reality
  - "Grass is green *because*..." - Reality w/ Logic
- Reality only gives us facts that we can touch, smell, see and hear. Logic gives us facts that go further.
  - Reality - tangible facts and experiences
  - Logic - intangible/abstract facts, theories
- How Logic manifests:
  - Logical soundness, what makes sense, avoidance of fallacies
  - Abstract/intangible facts and theories (math is a great example)
    - By extension, making abstract connections (which is in line with Intuition from Jung's theories)
  - Objective opinions, that's a MAJOR manifestation
    - Wait, is that even a thing? If so, then yeah
    - Actually, this is probably like opinions that are free of any Valuation influence
      - "He is an inefficient leader" vs. "He is a bad leader"
      - "He's a good basketball player because his stats are good" vs. "He's a good basketball player because he works so hard"
      - Opinions based on verifiable evidence vs. opinions based on personal preference
      - Saying that something is good even if you don't like it, or that something is bad even if you like it

## The Force of Imagination (M)

It should not be ignored that the world is a constantly changing place. Indeed, there have been many instances of discovery where something we thought was true was not, and vice versa. While the world presents to us several changes of its own, it is through the innovation of our own minds that we change the world ourselves. Though we do not necessarily need to be creative to change the world, many revolutionaries possessed wonderful imaginations, a word that forms the basis behind the following Force.

The **Force of Imagination** combines the Human Unit of the Heart and the Nonhuman Pole of Objection. It involves two major concepts:

- **Human Element:** The formation, understanding, and implementation of **one's own novel ideas and concepts, free from personal meaning or significance.**
- **Nonhuman Element:** Knowing and understanding **how one's world could or will change, free from what it was or currently is.**

**Unorganized thoughts**
- How Imagination manifests:
  - Coming up with new ideas, obviously
    - Devising something that has never been seen before in reality
  - Predicting the future; what could or will happen
    - Or, if Imagination is high enough, changing the future to one's own liking
  - Devising or latching onto a concept (with or without regard for its value); this is not engagement with the *value* of the concept *but with the concept itself*
- Reality deals with the world as is; Imagination is about the world's *changes*
- Ideas themselves don't have value per se; however, it often happens; that's why Imagination and Valuation are complementary Forces

## The Force of Valuation (V)

All non-sentient living creatures basically only have two thoughts in mind at all times: *survive* and *reproduce*. However, as sentient beings, we humans have developed the capacity to think about how we *feel* about the things in our lives. Whether things make us cry, smile, laugh, or most importantly, *if the things we have or do are even worth having or doing.* Every human needs a motivation or something to keep themselves going, otherwise we will end up miserable. There are certain things in our lives that we hold near and dear to our hearts; another way to put this is, we give them *value.*

The **Force of Valuation** combines the Human Unit of the Heart and the Nonhuman Pole of Subjection. It involves two major concepts:

- **Human Element:** The formation, understanding, and implementation of **one's own values, beliefs, possessions, concepts, ideas, etc. that carry personal significance, importance and meaning, free from change or novelty.**
- **Nonhuman Element:** The formation of **one's own subjective opinions based on personal preference, free from established rules or facts.**

**Unorganized thoughts**
- How Valuation manifests:
  - Putting meaning or personal significance in something
  - Moral values and systems; what we think is morally right
  - Subjective opinions is a MAJOR manifestation
    - What I mean is, opinions that stem from personal preferences

## The Force of Optimization (O)

### What Optimization is NOT

The **Force of Optimization** combines the Human Unit of the Body and the Equator of the Nonhuman Axis. Because of its relative uniqueness compared to the other four Forces just by this combination alone, there are several nuances that make it different on a fundamental level, so much so that this section *must* come first.

As stated, Optimization is the most unique of them all. You can tell right away just by the name and mechanisms I stated earlier; while the other four Forces determine the "what" and the "why," Optimization appears to deal with the "how," or the method one takes to get to their goals.

Before I get any further, I want you to take a moment to think about what the Force of Optimization *could* entail. Perhaps it is a way of finding the best possible solutions to any given problem. Or maybe it deals with efficiency and order, and organizing one's environment. It could be volitional, and unlike Imagination deal with goal-setting in its entirety, or it encompasses willpower and the indomitable human spirit.

Now I say to you: whatever I just threw out there, **the Force of Optimization involves NONE of it.** Any type can be quick or slow, efficient or inefficient when it comes to solving problems in their daily lives. Any type can be neat or sloppy, chaotic or orderly, or active or lazy. And most definitely, any type can have willpower and set goals, or not even know what to do with their lives.

In fact, Optimization is the only one of the Five Forces **that CANNOT be expressed by itself.**
